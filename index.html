DASTAAR

Taking my family's 50-year-old turban house in Jalandhar online. I owned it end to end: research, product, design and build.

The problem

Tehal Singh & Sons Pagri House has sold turbans in Jalandhar since 1973, trusted by families across Punjab and abroad. But everything happened over the counter: if you couldn't walk in, you couldn't buy.

Customers in other cities or abroad had to ask relatives to shop for them and guess colours over a phone call.

How might welet anyone, anywhere, buy the exact turban they want from a shop in Jalandhar?

Constraints I worked with

  • No team: one person for product, design and build
  • Colour is the product, and screens distort colour
  • Turbans are sold by the metre, so the price depends on fabric, length and stitching
  • Many buyers live abroad, far from the shop
  • A new online name has to borrow 50 years of trust

UX decisions

The six calls that shaped the site, and why I made them.

Colour is the menu

People ask for a shade, not a product code. So colour drives the home page, the filters and the recommendations.

In the journey: steps 2 and 4

A price that builds itself

Turbans are sold by the metre. The page multiplies rate by length and adds stitching, live: ₹95 × 5.5 m = ₹522.5.

In the journey: step 3

Verify, then call

First-time online buyers trust a person. WhatsApp verification plus a confirmation call keeps fake orders out and gives buyers a human touch.

In the journey: steps 5 and 6

Heritage above the fold

A new website has no reviews. The shop's 50 years, real photos and "hand-finished in Jalandhar" do that job instead.

In the journey: step 1

Grow the basket gently

Pins and gift wrap are things buyers already need. One tick each, shown after the turban is built.

In the journey: step 4

Sell the season

Festivals drive turban gifting. The Rakhi campaign takes the top of the home page, with ready boxes or a build-your-own option.

In the journey: step 2

The Explore page

A top consumer lending firm's app had 52M+ users who opened it to pay an EMI and left. I designed a page to make them stay and discover.

My roleProduct management, Digital Platforms
TimelineApr to Jun 2025
TeamProduct, design, tech, marketing, SEO
Research27 interviews, 94 surveys
ProblemResearchUsersJourneyPagesFlowsImpact

The problem

The app gives one-touch access to 50+ financial products. But most users only touched the one product they already had: pay the EMI, check the loan, close the app.

Business saw a super app. Users saw a service app. That gap meant missed cross-sell, under-used products and a purely transactional experience, while rivals were turning into super apps.

How might weturn the app from a place to pay EMIs into a place users come back to discover?

Goals I set

  • Raise product discovery with one central hub
  • Raise engagement with personalised, bite-sized content
  • Keep it calm: no clutter, no endless scroll
  • Protect business KPIs like cross-sell while doing it

Research

I used the Double Diamond to move from open research to a focused design.

Discover27 interviews in branches, electronics stores and on the phone; 94 surveys
DefineAffinity mapping into pain points and problem statements
DevelopFeature ideas and wireframes, tested with users and 8+ business owners
